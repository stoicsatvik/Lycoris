# What a hypothetical human actually experiences in a Mars mission

**This is an evidence-informed exposure map, not a forecast for a specific person.**

## 1. Preparation and launch
Physical stress and acceleration; vestibular adaptation; schedule displacement; acute task stress; fluid/hydration changes.

## 2. Outbound cruise, reduced load
Fluid redistribution, vestibular and sensorimotor adjustment, reduced skeletal loading, altered exercise demands, sleep/circadian strain, potentially changing cardiovascular and immune parameters. Radiation exposure depends on actual particle field and protection.

## 3. Mars landing, ~0.38g
Re-adaptation to body loading and locomotion, postural regulation, potential orthostatic symptoms and fatigue after transit. No published long-duration human 0.38g observation dataset establishes exact recovery curves.

## 4. Living inside a habitat
Sleep/wake cycles under Mars sols, food and water availability, exercise countermeasures, interpersonal conflict/cooperation, infection and immune monitoring, medical autonomy when Earth communication is delayed, mental well-being and cognitive workload.

## 5. Exterior excursions
Locomotion in reduced gravity plus terrain; handling samples; visual/hand/foot tasks; physical exertion and heat; breathing and CO2 removal demands that will couple to the *later suit*; falls, dust contamination, communication workload and mission planning errors.

## 6. Emergencies
Injury, illness, hypoxia/hypercapnia scenarios, decompression, thermal stress, exhaustion, time-critical decision making, response with delayed Earth consultation. All hazards require explicit scenario definitions and clinically validated criteria.

## 7. Long-term and return
Musculoskeletal and cardiovascular adaptation, changes in vision and sensory orientation, psychological response, recovery, long-term health surveillance. Long-latency radiation outcomes must be probabilistic and derived from defensible exposure-response evidence, not guessed per-EVA risk percentages.

## NASA's five hazard families
Altered gravity; radiation; isolation/confinement; distance from Earth; hostile/closed environments. Their effects may interact, making simplistic independent risk addition scientifically inappropriate.
