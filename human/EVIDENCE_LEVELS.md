# Human evidence transfer and confidence levels

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Direct human data, precise physiology and exposure context, quality controlled; still limited to original setting |
| B | Human data from another environment (ISS, Moon, bed-rest, analog), with an explicit transfer model |
| C | Validated terrestrial laboratory/clinical measurement but no accepted Mars transfer |
| D | Mechanistic hypothesis or expert assumption not independently calibrated |
| U | No adequate evidence for a numerical prediction |

These are **Lycoris proposed evidence tags**, not NASA's official risk grades. Assign source-specific evidence per claim/parameter/model, not a single label to an entire organ.

- Microgravity observations cannot directly calibrate long-duration human effects at 0.38g.
- Lunar EVA operations had approximately one-sixth Earth gravity for limited mission durations; Mars differs in gravity, dust, environment and mission history.
- Earth confinement analogs validate psychological/operational context only, not Martian radiation or gravity.
- In a model with multiple uncertain factors, carry correlation and parameter covariance rather than pretending all organ responses are independent.
- Never infer psychological diagnosis from task errors, biometrics, or video alone. Do not interpret association as deterministic cause.
